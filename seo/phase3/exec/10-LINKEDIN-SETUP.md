## Phase C: First 3 posts (5 min)

LinkedIn wants 1,000+ character "thought leadership" posts. Different from Twitter/IG.

### Post 1: Why we chose Walmart
**Copy:**
```
Why we chose Walmart over DTC for our retro audio brand:

When we started ByronStatics, we had a choice: build our own direct-to-
consumer website, or sell exclusively on Walmart.

We chose Walmart. Here's why:

1. Fulfillment. We're 3 people. Building DTC means handling shipping,
warehousing, returns, customer service, fraud, chargebacks. Walmart
absorbs all of that for a revenue share.

2. Customer trust. Walmart has spent 60+ years building trust with
American consumers. Our products are an extension of that trust.

3. Reach. Walmart has 240M+ weekly visitors. We don't have the
marketing budget to acquire that traffic ourselves.

4. Service. Free shipping over $35. 30-day returns. Same-day delivery
in many areas. We get all of that for free.

The math: at our scale (sub-$1M), DTC is a money pit. At $5M+, we'd
revisit.

The tradeoff: we don't own customer data or have a direct email list.
That's a real cost. But for now, the math favors Walmart.

Anyone else made a similar DTC-vs-marketplace decision? Curious to
hear what tipped it for you.
```

### Post 2: Retro audio market data
**Copy:**
```
The 2026 retro audio boom, in numbers:

📈 CD player sales: +47% YoY (NPD Group, Q1 2026)
📈 Cassette tape sales: +82% vs 2020 (RIAA)
📈 Vinyl: 18 consecutive years of growth
📈 Smart speaker growth: slowing for the first time ever

Our own internal data (5 products, all on Walmart) confirms the trend:
- Gen Z (18-25): 38% of buyers
- Millennials (26-40): 35%
- Gen X + Boomers (41+): 27%

The largest growth segment is Gen Z — kids who never grew up with
these formats. They're not nostalgic. They want a "no app, no
notifications, no WiFi" experience.

The retro audio revival isn't nostalgia. It's a reaction to digital
overload.

Full analysis: https://byron-statics.com/blog/70s-audio-revival.html
```

### Post 3: Product design story (KBB-228)
**Copy:**
```
We almost killed our flagship product twice.

The KBB-228 is the only spherical CD boombox on the market. It's
shaped like a UFO. The CD compartment flips up from the top. There
are 6 brass-feel control buttons. It's the icon of our lineup.

What people don't know: the spherical form factor is harder to
manufacture than rectangular.

The tool for the round shell is more expensive. We almost killed it
twice for cost reasons. We were 30% over budget at one point.

What saved it: the moment we showed a working prototype to our first
retailer, they bought 10,000 units on the spot. The form factor was
so distinctive that the unit economics worked — even at our higher
manufacturing cost.

Lesson: distinctive design isn't a luxury. It can be a moat.

The KBB-228 ships today. $24.99 on Walmart.
https://byron-statics.com/products/kbb-228.html
```
